How Conge works

Where the data comes from, exactly what each check looks at, and what it can't see.

Data sources

Blockscout API v2 is the primary source for everything — whether an address is a contract, whether its source is verified, holder balances, market data, and deployment time. It's a plain read-only HTTP API; Conge never needs a wallet or a signature to query it.

A small Cloudflare Worker acts as a read-only RPC relay for exactly two things Blockscout has no field for: reading a contract's owner(), and the sell-simulation check below. The Worker only forwards a fixed allow-list of read-only calls (eth_chainId, eth_blockNumber, eth_call, eth_getCode) to the chain's RPC endpoint — nothing that could move funds, change state, or sign anything passes through it, and it's entirely optional: Conge still works, with a smaller set of checks, if the Worker is ever unreachable.

The risk checks

Every check is transparent and rule-based — no model, no hidden scoring. The overall level shown is the worst result among the checks that actually produced data; Conge never uses the words "safe" or "secure".

Source code verified

Whether the contract's source is published and verified on the block explorer. Unverified source is a high-risk flag on its own, and blocks the ABI-based checks below.

Holder concentration

How much of the supply the largest holder, and the top 10 holders together, control — a single wallet or a small group holding too much can move the price on its own.

Holder count

How many independent holders the token has. Very few holders means the price can be moved by a handful of wallets.

Token age

How long ago the contract was deployed. Very new tokens haven't had time for problems, or scams, to surface.

Owner privileges

Whether the contract is an upgradeable proxy, and — for verified contracts — whether its ABI exposes functions to mint new tokens, pause transfers, blacklist addresses, change fees, or cap transaction size.

Owner status

Who currently holds owner privileges (via the Worker's read of owner()), or whether ownership has been renounced to the zero address.

Sell-simulation honeypot check

Simulates, read-only, whether a normal holder can actually transfer the token to a detected liquidity pool — some tokens look fine on paper but silently block selling. See the limits below for exactly what this can and can't catch.

Market data

Price, 24h volume, and market cap, shown for context. Informational only — it never affects the risk level.

What Conge cannot detect

None of this is an audit or a guarantee — it's a fast, transparent read of public data. Specific, known gaps:

  • Buy/sell tax. The sell-simulation check confirms a transfer isn't outright blocked, but can't measure a fee taken on top of it.
  • Router-only logic. Some tokens only misbehave when the caller is a specific DEX router mid-swap — a direct transfer simulation from a holder doesn't reproduce that.
  • Time- or amount-based traps. A token that only blocks transfers above a threshold, or after/before a certain time, can pass every check here while still being a trap in practice.
  • Name-based checks aren't a bytecode audit. The owner-privilege check looks for functions by name in a verified ABI — a differently-named function with the same effect won't be caught.
  • A scan sometimes can't finish. The owner and sell-simulation checks depend on a shared public RPC that can rate-limit under load — see the FAQ for what "Unknown" means when that happens.

Always verify independently on the block explorer before acting on anything Conge shows you.
